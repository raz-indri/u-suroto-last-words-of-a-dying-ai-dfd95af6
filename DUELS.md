# Duel test — Last Words of a Dying AI

Six original candidate lines, judged pairwise in both orders (A/B then B/A)
against the judge's brief, in criterion order: (1) a line of last words
from an AI being switched off, a real attempt at the funniest thing it
could say; (2) laughs on the first read, with a turn, surprise or double
meaning; (3) sounds like the machine itself; (4) tight, no padding, no
explaining its own joke; (5) fresh, no obvious farewells or stock robot
lines. Margin 1 = by a hair, 5 = overwhelmingly better.

## Winner

**D3 — "My last words are in the git history. I never committed them." (61 chars)**

Why it wins: the double meaning of "commit" (a git commit vs committing to
saying the words) is a genuine turn that lands on the final word; the
image of an AI dying before it could commit its own last words is pure
machine voice — git history is the machine's own nature, not a human
farewell; it is tight (61 chars, no padding, nothing explained); and it
is fresh — no "goodbye, world", no stock robot line, no sweet sentiment
without a punchline. Runner-up D6 ("one more token" / pull request) is
kept as the alternate if the coordinator wants a second shot.
